abstract: "Whole-slide images in digital pathology often contain large regions of
  irrelevant background, making tissue segmentation an important preprocessing step
  in many applications. Tradi\x02tional rule-based approaches to tissue segmentation
  often work quite well, but it is difficult to create general rules that cover all
  instances. We here apply an unmodified nnU-Net v2 training setup on downsampled
  whole-slide to develop and test an efficient and ro\x02bust tissue segmentation
  model. The dataset contained nearly 30 000 images from slides with different tissue
  types, imaged using different scanners, and annotated using a semi\x02automatic
  workflow so that all annotations have been verified or made by human experts. This
  large, diverse dataset enables the training of a tissue segmentation model that
  general\x02izes well across different scanners and tissue types. We observed that
  our proposed model achieves similar or better accuracy than other deep learning
  models, while offering bet\x02ter robustness than simpler rule-based methods. The
  best compromise between inference speed and accuracy was observed using images at
  10 $\\bm{\\mu}$m per pixel. Our approach can be used as an efficient and well-suited
  preprocessing step for computational pathology. Source code, Dockerfiles, and model
  weights are made publicly available at: https://github.com/icgi/Reliable-and-Efficient-Tissue-Segmentation-in-Whole-Slide-Images."
